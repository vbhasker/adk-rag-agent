The big picture: what is RAG?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation means: before the LLM answers, you retrieve the relevant bits of your own data and paste them into the prompt. The model then answers from those facts instead of from its (possibly outdated or hallucinated) memory.

Coach says90% of "the LLM gave a bad answer" bugs in RAG are actually "we retrieved the wrong text" bugs. The G is easy. The R is where careers are made. So we spend 7 chapters on R and 1 on making the agent clever.

Our knowledge base for the whole course is Nimbus Bikes, a made-up e-bike company with 12 docs: specs, warranty, battery care, error codes, returns. You'll find it in data/docs/.

Step 1 · Tokenization: turning text into countable things

Computers can't compare meaning (yet, that's Chapter 2), but they can compare tokens. Our tokenizer does three things:

  1. Lowercase + split on anything that isn't a letter or digit, but keeps codes like e-07, 4.2.1 and un3480 intact.
  2. Drop stop words (the, is, my…): they appear everywhere, so they carry almost no signal.
  3. Stem: chop suffixes so charge, charges, charging and charged all become charg.

Step 2 · The inverted index

Instead of scanning every document for every query, search engines flip the data around: for each token, keep the list of documents containing it (plus counts). That list is called a postings list, and the whole thing is an inverted index. It's the same idea as the index at the back of a book.

TokenPostings (doc → count)Document frequency (df)
batterybattery-care → 15, troubleshooting-power → 13, model-cumulus-cargo → 6, …10 of 12 docs, common, weak signal
e-07error-codes → 11 of 12 docs, rare, strong signal
refundreturns-shipping → 41 of 12 docs

Our Python version keeps a Counter per document, which is fine for 12 docs. Elasticsearch/OpenSearch, Lucene, Tantivy and Postgres full-text search keep real inverted indexes that scale to billions.

Step 3 · TF-IDF: the grandparent

Two intuitions, multiplied together:

TF: term frequency

A doc that says "battery" 15 times is probably about batteries.

IDF: inverse document frequency

A word in every doc ("bike") tells you nothing. A word in one doc ("E-07") is gold.

score(D, Q) = Σt∈Q tf(t, D) × idf(t)

Two problems: (1) saying "battery" 40 times makes a doc 40× more relevant, which is easy to spam and just wrong; (2) long documents win simply because they contain more words.

Step 4 · BM25: TF-IDF with good manners

score(D, Q) = Σt∈Q IDF(t) · tf · (k1 + 1)tf + k1 · (1 − b + b · |D| / avgdl)

k1: saturation (default 1.2 – 2.0)

The 1st mention of a word matters a lot, the 10th barely at all. Score approaches a ceiling instead of growing forever. k1 = 0 ignores frequency entirely.

b: length normalisation (default 0.75)

Penalise documents longer than average (avgdl). b = 0 ignores length; b = 1 fully normalises.

IDF uses the Lucene/Elasticsearch flavour: ln(1 + (N − df + 0.5) / (df + 0.5)), which is always positive. That's the exact formula in bm25.py.

2026 best practices for keyword search

  • BM25 isn't legacy, it's a teammate. Nearly every serious RAG stack runs BM25 alongside vectors (Chapter 4) because it nails IDs, SKUs, error codes, names and acronyms that embeddings blur.
  • Don't hand-roll it in production. Use Elasticsearch/OpenSearch, Postgres full-text search (or the pg_search/ParadeDB BM25 extension), Tantivy, or a managed service like Vertex AI Search. We hand-rolled it here to understand it.
  • Get the analyzer right: language-specific stemming, stop words, synonym lists (ebike ⇄ e-bike), and keeping codes as single tokens matter more than tuning k1/b.
  • Field boosting (BM25F): a hit in the title is usually worth more than a hit in the body.
  • Learned sparse retrieval (e.g. SPLADE) keeps the inverted-index speed but learns which extra terms to add ("bike won't start" → also "power", "battery"). Worth knowing it exists.
PitfallsTokenizers that split E-07 into e + 07; forgetting to apply the same tokenizer to queries and docs; judging BM25 on a 12-doc corpus (IDF gets far more meaningful with thousands of docs).
