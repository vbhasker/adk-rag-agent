What is an embedding?

An embedding model turns text into a fixed-length list of numbers (a vector): 384 numbers for bge-small, 768 to 3,072 for gemini-embedding-001. Think of it as GPS coordinates for meaning: texts that mean similar things get coordinates that are close together.

How does it learn meaning? Through contrastive training on hundreds of millions of pairs (question ↔ answer, title ↔ body, query ↔ clicked result). The model is rewarded for pulling matching pairs together and pushing random pairs apart. After enough of that, "won't turn on" and "stays dark" end up neighbours.

Sparse vectors (Chapter 1)

  • One dimension per word in the vocabulary (tens of thousands)
  • Almost all zeros
  • Exact-match, explainable
  • Blind to synonyms

Dense vectors (this chapter)

  • Few hundred to few thousand dimensions
  • Every number used
  • Match meaning, paraphrases and even other languages
  • A black box; fuzzy on exact codes

The one fact to rememberNormalise every vector to length 1 and then cosine = dot and euclidean² = 2 − 2·cosine. All three give the identical ranking. That's why embeddings.py normalises everything once, and why vector databases happily use the cheapest option, the dot product. Try it in the playground: switch metrics and the order doesn't budge.

Three 2026 details that trip people up

Asymmetric retrieval

A 7-word question and a 300-word answer look different. Good models embed them differently on purpose: Gemini takes task_type=RETRIEVAL_QUERY vs RETRIEVAL_DOCUMENT; bge adds a query instruction. That's why embed_query() ≠ embed_documents().

Matryoshka dimensions

gemini-embedding-001 outputs 3,072 dims, but it's trained so the first 768 (or 1,536) still work well. 4× less storage for a small quality drop. Truncated vectors must be re-normalised (we do).

Scores aren't probabilities

With bge-small, "bike" vs "banana" scores 0.60, "bike" vs "bicycle" 0.95. Every model has its own range. Never hard-code "relevant if > 0.7" without measuring (Chapter 7).

  2. Compare I love my bike / I do not love my bike: 0.83. Embeddings are notoriously weak at negation. A reranker (Chapter 5) handles this better.
  3. Compare battery / Batterie (German). Multilingual models like Gemini's do much better here than English-only bge-small.
  4. Search the same query with all four metrics. The ranking never changes because the vectors are normalised. Scores do.
  5. Search UN3480 and firmware 4.2.1. Check whether semantic search still lands the right doc, and by how much.
  6. Set EMBEDDINGS_PROVIDER=toy and restart. The hashing embedder shows what "vectors without meaning" look like: spelling overlap only.

The new problem this chapter creates

We squeezed each whole document (up to ~300 words) into one vector. A doc about warranty periods and exclusions and how to claim becomes an average of all three topics, a blurry centroid. A specific question like "is a speed chip covered?" has to compete with that blur. Worse, real documents are 50 pages, far past an embedding model's input limit (512 tokens for bge-small, 2,048 for gemini-embedding-001).

2026 best practices for embeddings

  • Picking a model: on Google Cloud, gemini-embedding-001 (multilingual, Matryoshka 768/1536/3072) is the default choice. Open-weight options include EmbeddingGemma (small enough for on-device), the BGE, E5, Nomic and Qwen3-Embedding families. Use the MTEB leaderboard to shortlist, then test on your own data (Chapter 7).
  • Use task types / instructions: query vs document embeddings are one of the cheapest quality wins available.
  • Store the model name + dimension with every vector. Mixing vectors from two models is a silent bug: nothing crashes, everything is just wrong. Changing models means re-embedding everything.
  • Cache embeddings (we do, in .cache/): re-running ingestion shouldn't re-bill you.
  • Normalise and use dot product unless your model's docs say otherwise.
PitfallsThresholding raw similarity scores; embedding queries with the document task type; forgetting that embeddings have an input limit and silently truncate long text; assuming "semantic" means "understands negation and numbers".
